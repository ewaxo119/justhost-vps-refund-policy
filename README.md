## When the 15 days start, and what counts as a day

JustVPS publishes the clearest wording of the group: a customer may cancel and request a refund "only within 15 (fifteen) calendar days from the date of the relevant payment." Calendar days, counted from the payment date, not from provisioning and not from your first login. Pay on the 3rd and the window shuts on the 18th, weekends included. The request has to go through their refund form with a stated reason.

gghost.cloud words the same idea as "within 15 days of its first payment," which anchors the clock in the same place but adds two limits: the guarantee covers the first payment for a new service and is available once per customer. It explicitly does not cover renewals.

FireVPS goes further in a different direction. Its 15 days apply to shared hosting, business hosting, reseller hosting, Windows RDP and VPS packages, but only on annual billing. Monthly or any other term gets 7 days.

So the three words in the search phrase hide three separate variables: the start date, calendar versus business days, and whether the billing term changes the answer. Two weeks is 14 days, so nobody selling you "15 business days" would be giving you three weeks; none of the policies above use business days for the window itself.

JustHost publishes no such window at all. The only timelines in its refund documentation are procedural: 14 days to issue the refund, up to 45 days for the credit to appear.

## What a 15-day promise usually doesn't cover

The exclusions do more work than the window. Across the four policies examined here:

- **Rule violations.** JustVPS refuses refunds where the service was used for spam or unsolicited bulk mail, fraud, phishing, malware hosting, attacks on networks, hacking tools, unlicensed software distribution, or IP infringement. gghost excludes services suspended or ended for breaking its Acceptable Use rules. FireVPS excludes accounts it terminated for Terms or AUP violations from both the 7-day and 15-day guarantee.
- **Domains and IP addresses.** JustVPS makes domain registration and renewal payments and IPv4/IPv6 lease payments non-refundable. FireVPS excludes domain registration, transfer and renewal, SSL certificates and dedicated IPs. JustHost's own refund article states that refunds for domain registration and network rental are not possible.
- **Setup fees and dedicated hardware.** JustVPS charges a dedicated server setup fee equal to 50% of the server price, one-off and non-refundable, with the remainder potentially refundable inside the 15 days. A $500 server therefore has at most $250 coming back. FireVPS offers no refunds on dedicated servers, custom packages, setup fees, advanced support fees, processing fees, software licences, or bundled WHMCS and cPanel licences. UltaHost excludes dedicated servers and VDS above $96.50.
- **The billing term you chose.** UltaHost's VPS section promises a full refund inside 30 days, then states that refunds are not available when cancelling a yearly, biennial or triennial VPS plan. Its Mac VPS line gets 15 days. Read those two sentences together before you assume the longer period applies to the plan you want.
- **How you paid.** UltaHost refuses refunds on purchases made with cryptocurrency or other digital assets, by cheque, money order or bank wire, and on plans bought with a promo code or discount link. FireVPS handles bank wire, Western Union, cheque and money order payments as account credit rather than money back.
- **Multiple orders.** UltaHost's example is specific: an account with more than one hosting order is not eligible, such as three VPS servers used for 20 days before a refund request.
- **Discretion.** FireVPS reserves the right to refuse a refund at any time for any or no reason. A 15-day guarantee that sits next to that clause is a statement of intent, not an enforceable promise.

The formal policy, revised 7 October 2026, fills in the accounting. Refunds go back through the original payment channel under that provider's rules, and where that's impossible another lawful method is used after entitlement is verified. Necessary and unavoidable expenses tied to a specific order can be deducted, provided their connection to the order and the permissibility of the deduction are confirmed separately; the company must take reasonable steps to cancel those expenses, and anything later recovered from third parties is paid to the customer within 10 calendar days. Dedicated server initial setup can be a separate one-off service, with its price disclosed before payment, deducted only for a separately agreed and actually performed scope of work, and never charged again on an ordinary renewal.

Read plainly, JustHost's refund route is closer to a remedy for a defective or unusable service than to a cooling-off period. Your case rests on documenting the problem, and any deduction has to be justified as a real cost. If you need a guaranteed exit after a trial, this isn't the provider designed for that.

One related distinction that trips people up elsewhere: turning off automatic renewal stops the next charge but returns nothing for the period you already paid. JustVPS states this explicitly, and it holds in practice across the hosts above. Cancelling and refunding are two different requests.

## FAQ

**Is the 15-day window in calendar or business days?**
JustVPS spells out calendar days. gghost counts 15 days from first payment without qualifying the type, and its 7-business-day figure refers to how fast they reply, not to how long you have. Treat the window as calendar days unless the policy says otherwise, and file early.

**Can I get a refund on an annual VPS?**
It depends on the host, and the answer often reverses the headline. FireVPS gives 15 days only to annual billing. UltaHost refuses refunds on yearly, biennial and triennial VPS plans despite advertising 30 days. gghost's 15 days apply to VPS and dedicated servers regardless of term, but only to the first payment.

**Do renewals qualify?**
gghost excludes renewals explicitly and limits the guarantee to the first payment for a new service. UltaHost limits its standard offer to the first package on billing accounts under 30 days old. Assume a renewal payment is not refundable until you find wording that says it is.

**I used it for 10 days and it didn't work out. Am I still eligible?**
Inside the window, generally yes, unless you breached the terms. FireVPS is stricter: refunds apply where the server environment is incompatible, can't provide the features your program needs, or staff can't solve the issue, and not where you used the service to test a script and cancelled afterwards. JustHost requires you to demonstrate a problem that prevents use and to let support attempt a fix.

**Does the money go back to my card?**
JustVPS refunds to the original bank account, card or wallet, and won't send money to third-party payment details. JustHost uses the original channel as a rule. UltaHost may refund to account balance if the original method isn't possible. FireVPS turns wire, Western Union, cheque and money order payments into hosting credit.

**What happens if I skip support and file a chargeback?**
UltaHost blocks the account, stops you buying or using its services again, may terminate stored data without warning, and charges fees for each chargeback including those from third parties; blatant payment fraud ends the account permanently. It asks you to contact support first and says all charges must be repaid in full if you file without doing so.

## Before you order

Match the plan to what you'd be happy keeping, because the refund is the weakest part of the deal, not the strongest.

- **You only want a short trial.** Choose a host that publishes a first-payment window and no setup fee: gghost covers VPS for 15 days once per customer, JustVPS gives 15 calendar days through a form. Budget for the processing time on top.
- **You want annual savings.** Check the term against the window first. FireVPS's 15 days exist only on annual billing, UltaHost excludes annual VPS entirely, and JustHost's 12-month rates save 20% with no window at all.
- **You're buying several servers.** Confirm the multi-order rule. UltaHost won't refund an account holding more than one hosting order, which makes a three-server test an all-or-nothing purchase.
